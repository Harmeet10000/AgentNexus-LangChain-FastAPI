The Pyramid vs The Trophy

≈10 minutes · Prerequisites: L1–L5 · Next: What NOT to Test (lesson 7)

You now own all three layers. Two respected maps disagree about how to weight them — and understanding why they disagree is what separates seniors from people who memorized a diagram.

The Test Pyramid

E2E — few
Integration — some
Unit — many

Vocke's restatement of Mike Cohn's classic [R2]. Logic: fast tests are cheap to run and pinpoint failures, so build wide at the base; expensive slow layers stay narrow.

The Testing Trophy

E2E — thin
Integration — the prize
Unit — smaller

Kent C. Dodds' counter-model [R3], quoting Guillermo Rauch: "write tests; not too many; mostly integration." Claim: integration checks deliver near-E2E confidence at near-unit cost, while heavy unit investment often just locks in implementation details — Lesson 3's trap at scale.

Where they actually agree

  • E2E stays scarce in both models — no serious practitioner argues otherwise
  • Behavior over implementation: both punish suites that break on refactoring
  • Fast feedback rules everything: slow suites die socially before they die technically

The real question underneath the disagreement

Your codebase looks like…Lean toward…
Rich domain logic: pricing engines, parsers, state machines, algorithmsPyramid — pure logic rewards unit-level breadth
Thin logic over infrastructure: CRUD services, API orchestration, glue codeTrophy — most risk lives AT the boundaries, so test there
Senior move: don't pick a religion — read your codebase. The shape follows where your risk lives. That judgment call, made consciously per project, IS senior-level test strategy.

Judging any suite in five minutes

  1. Count tests per layer — is the mass where the codebase's risk lives?
  2. Break one small behavior mentally — would failure localize precisely or vaguely?
  3. Ask the team: how often do refactors break tests that shouldn't care?
  4. Check CI wall-time — does anyone actually wait for green before merging?
  5. Look for quarantined/flaky tests — a growing pile means eroding trust [R4]
